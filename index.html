Desk by desk

What each desk does, and the rules the system enforces there.

Reception

  • Register with a generated MRN: name, sex, date of birth or estimated age, phone, address, national ID, next of kin and photo.
  • Duplicate check, and merge of two records of the same person.
  • Card fee and card validity; choice of room and doctor on duty.
  • Pediatrics refuses patients at or over the age limit (default 18).
  • Patient ID card with barcode; documents on the record (referrals, ID scans, outside reports).

Triage

  • Vitals with graded alerts: mild, moderate, critical.
  • Triage category and chief complaint.
  • Queue order: Red first, by category, or by arrival.
  • Triage can be switched off for facilities that do not use it.

Doctor

  • Dashboard of cases: checked in, waiting for results, results back.
  • Complaints, history, physical examination (Normal or Abnormal per body system), and diagnosis with ICD code and Confirmed or Presumed.
  • Lab and imaging orders, order sets, and prescriptions with an allergy check.
  • Results with flags and the panel table; acknowledge, then close the case.
  • Admission decision, printed prescription and discharge summary.
  • After closing, an addendum or reopening with a reason. The original is always kept.

Laboratory

  • Work list, workload board, specimen collection with label print. The specimen is taken only after payment.
  • Normal ranges by sex and age, and an automatic flag: Low, High, Critical or Abnormal.
  • Panels with one value per measurement: CBC, liver, kidney, urinalysis.
  • Reject a specimen with a reason; the doctor sees why.
  • Optional second-person validation, or send a result back. Referral laboratory send-out.
  • Correct a posted result: the old value stays visible.

Imaging

  • Orders, start of study, report.
  • Attach images, PDF or DICOM files up to 10 MB.

Pharmacy and store

  • Dispense from the prescription, by batch and expiry, after payment.
  • Stores, receipts, transfers between stores, stock count.
  • Reorder and expiry alerts; purchase orders.

Cashier

  • Payments, receipts, and a collections report with search.
  • Shifts: opening float, counted drawer, a reason for any difference.
  • Refunds with approval, discounts within a limit, owed balances.
  • CBHI, fee-waiver and exempt schemes.

Wards and emergency

  • Wards, rooms and beds; admit, transfer, discharge with summary.
  • Nursing notes, vitals chart, medication administration record.
  • Bed-day and theatre charges go to the bill.
  • Emergency quick registration, emergency board with bays, admission from the ER.

Theatre

  • Booking with a room-clash check; start, complete and theatre fee.
  • WHO safety checklist: sign in, time out, sign out.
  • Team, anaesthesia type and ASA class.
  • An operative note the surgeon signs. A signed note is locked.

Reports

  • Everyday reports: visits, ward census, money, lab turnaround, stock, top diagnoses.
  • HMIS monthly summary by age band and sex.
  • Report builder: count one thing grouped by up to two; reports can be saved by name.
  • Download as Excel or PDF.

Appointments

  • Clinic sessions with slots; doctor hours and approved leave respected.
  • Double booking is refused.
  • SMS reminders a set number of hours before. They need an SMS provider account.

Enterprise and patient

  • Insurance coverage and claims; finance journals; HR and payroll.
  • Patient portal and app: their own record, results and bookings; Amharic in the app.

Test it yourself

Short scenarios to run on the pilot system with your staff. Open each one for the steps and what you should see. Every scenario has an automated version that passed on 16 October 2026.

1. A new outpatient visit, reception to pharmacy
  1. Reception: register a new patient and take the card fee.
  2. Triage: enter vitals and a category.
  3. Doctor: open the case, write the complaint and examination, add a diagnosis and order a lab test.
  4. Cashier: take the lab fee. Laboratory: collect the specimen and post the result.
  5. Doctor: read the result, prescribe and close the case. Pharmacy: dispense.

Expected: each desk sees the patient only when it is their turn, the lab cannot collect before payment, and the stock of the dispensed drug goes down.

2. Lab flags from normal ranges
  1. Order a CBC for a woman, pay, and collect the specimen.
  2. Enter hemoglobin 9, white cells 40 and platelets 200.
  3. Post the panel.

Expected: while you type, hemoglobin shows Low, white cells Critical and platelets Normal. The panel is flagged Critical. The same hemoglobin of 13 shows Low for a man and Normal for a woman.

3. Reject a specimen
  1. Collect a specimen, then choose Reject specimen and the reason Haemolysed.
  2. Open the same case as the doctor.

Expected: the order goes back to waiting for a specimen. The doctor sees "Specimen rejected: Haemolysed". Collecting again does not charge the patient twice.

4. Second-person validation of lab results
  1. Administrator: in Facility workflow, choose "A second person validates each result".
  2. Lab technician A posts a result. The doctor opens the case.
  3. Technician A tries to validate it, then technician B validates it.

Expected: the doctor sees "waiting" until it is validated. A cannot validate their own result. After B validates, the doctor sees the value and who validated it.

5. Correct a signed record
  1. The lab corrects a posted result and gives a reason.
  2. The doctor adds an addendum to a closed case, then reopens a case closed by mistake.

Expected: the old value stays visible next to the new one, with who, when and why. A nurse cannot add an addendum.

6. Admission, ward care and discharge
  1. The doctor decides to admit. The ward assigns a bed.
  2. The nurse charts vitals and gives a medicine on the medication chart.
  3. Transfer to another bed, then discharge with a summary.

Expected: the bed shows occupied, bed days are charged, the discharge summary prints, and the bed is free again.

7. Theatre with the WHO safety checklist
  1. Administrator: make the theatre checklist required.
  2. Book a case and try to start it. Tick Sign in and Time out, then start.
  3. Record the team, anaesthesia and the operative note. Tick Sign out, sign the note, then complete.

Expected: the case cannot start before Sign in and Time out, and cannot be completed before Sign out and a signed note. After signing, the note cannot be edited.

8. Cashier shift and refund
  1. Open a shift with a float and take some payments.
  2. Request a refund; approve it as finance or the administrator.
  3. Close the shift and count the drawer.

Expected: the shift report shows expected against counted cash, and a difference needs a reason. A discount over the cashier's limit is refused.

9. Appointments and SMS reminders
  1. Open a clinic session with 30-minute slots. Book a slot, then try to book the same slot again.
  2. Try to book the doctor outside their hours, or on approved leave.
  3. Administrator: turn on SMS reminders 24 hours ahead and press "Send due reminders now".

Expected: the double booking, out-of-hours and leave bookings are refused. With an SMS provider set up, patients with a phone receive one reminder each.

10. HMIS monthly summary and the report builder
  1. Reports, then "HMIS monthly summary and custom reports". Pick a month and press Show.
  2. Download it as Excel and as PDF.
  3. Build a report: count visits by sex and age band. Save it by name.

Expected: the age and sex tables add up to the totals, the visit total matches the everyday report, and both files open.

11. Accounts and sign-in security
  1. Administrator: open an account with a weak password, then a good one.
  2. Sign in as that person. Then enter a wrong password 5 times.
  3. Administrator: unlock the account, then set a new password for it.

Expected: the weak password is refused, the person must choose their own password at first sign-in, the account locks after 5 wrong tries, and unlock works.

12. Backup and restore (IT staff)
  1. Run the nightly backup by hand.
  2. Run the monthly restore check.

Expected: an encrypted backup file is written and opened straight away to prove it can be read. The restore check reports OK, with the same row counts as the live system.

What your administrator controls

These change how the hospital works without a programmer.

Workflow switches

  • Card fee on or off, and when it is charged.
  • Triage required or skipped; queue order.
  • Pay before service, or bill later.
  • What CBHI, fee-waiver and exempt patients are spared.
  • Required registration fields; Pediatrics age limit.
  • Admission by doctor decision first, or direct.
  • Settle the bill before discharge; cashier shifts required; discount limit.
  • Lab second-person validation; theatre checklist required.
  • SMS reminders and how many hours ahead.

Access and catalogs

  • Roles and permissions per desk; one named account per person.
  • Lockout after 5 wrong passwords; unlock; set a password that must be changed.
  • Prices and services, lab tests and their normal ranges, drugs, rooms, wards and stores.
  • Bulk import of catalogs.

Safety, records and operations

Records

  • Every change is in the audit trail, with who and when.
  • Signed records are never overwritten. Corrections are added beside the original.
  • A critical lab result is texted to the ordering doctor once an SMS provider is set up.

Server

  • Runs on your own server and network; no internet needed for daily work.
  • Encrypted nightly backup, kept 30 days; monthly restore check.
  • Health check for "not answering", a disk 85% full, and a missing backup.
  • Demo accounts are switched off at installation.

  • Registration, IDs and the patient record; queue and triage.
  • Consultation, orders, prescriptions with allergy check, order sets.
  • Laboratory with ranges, panels, validation, rejection and referral.
  • Pharmacy and stock; wards, nursing and the medication chart.
  • Billing with cashier controls; appointments with SMS.
  • Theatre with the WHO checklist; everyday and HMIS-style reports; patient portal.

What Bahmni has that we do not yet

  • TB, HIV and ANC programs; immunisation schedules.
  • Form builder, coded observations, calculated fields; SNOMED, LOINC and CIEL.
  • Staff screens in Amharic (the patient app already has Amharic).
  • Lab analyser interface, microbiology, quality control.
  • PACS and DICOM viewer; radiology report templates.
  • Drug interaction check, procedure orders, growth chart, back-dated visits.
  • Fluid balance, expected discharge date, undo discharge.
  • Controlled-drug register, return to stock, VAT lines, supplier bills.
  • Recurring appointments, theatre calendar, e-mailed reports, fingerprint ID.

Road to go-live

1
Hospital server in place

About 8 cores, 32 GB memory, two mirrored SSDs of at least 500 GB, a UPS and a backup disk. A starting point, not a quotation.

2
Installation and your data

We install the system, then import your price list, test and drug catalogs, and staff list. A named administrator; one account per staff member.

3
Training

Desk leads are trained desk by desk, with short desk guides.

4
Acceptance test

Your staff run the scenarios on this page, plus a load test and a security review on your server.

5
Pilot go-live

Outpatient plus one ward first, then the rest of the hospital.

Optional: an SMS provider account turns on appointment reminders and critical-result texts.
