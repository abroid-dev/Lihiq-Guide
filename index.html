02 · Police hospital

Free care for members, counted against the government budget

Members are treated without paying. Every service still carries its full price, so the hospital can show what the care cost, month by month and for the Ethiopian fiscal year.

How payment is waived

1
The administrator switches free care on

On the Facility workflow page, with the name of the sponsor, for example Federal Police. Every change of the switch is in the audit trail.

2
Reception registers the patient

Reception does not ask for a payment mode. The card fee is charged to the sponsor at the full price, and the patient goes straight to triage without visiting the cashier.

3
Every service is charged to the sponsor

Lab tests, imaging, medicine, bed days and theatre are priced from the hospital price list and charged to the sponsor. The laboratory collects the specimen and the pharmacy dispenses without waiting for a receipt.

4
Nothing is given away without a record

Each charge keeps its full value and its date. The free-care report adds them up by service, member type and unit.

Note: while the switch is on, it applies to every patient in the hospital. If the hospital will also see paying civilians, tell us (question 2 below) and we will make it apply only to members.

The member record

  • Police ID number, rank, unit, and member type: serving, retired, spouse, child or civilian staff. The administrator can add or rename types.
  • The date the member card is valid until.
  • A spouse or child is linked to the principal member, found by police ID, and follows that member's card.
  • Each patient shows as Verified, Card expired, Not verified or Not recorded.
  • A patient whose card is expired or not verified is still treated, but reception must write the reason, for example "Renewal letter seen".

The free-care report

  • Pick a month, or a whole Ethiopian fiscal year (Hamle 1 to Sene 30, about 8 July to 7 July).
  • Value of care by service, by member type and by unit.
  • The list of patients treated without a verified card, with each reason.
  • Medicines issued, valued at the latest purchase price.
  • Purchases grouped by budget code.
  • Download as Excel or PDF. It is under Reports, "Free care and budget".

Budget codes on purchases

  • The store or finance keeps the list of budget codes from the Ministry of Finance chart of accounts in Procurement.
  • Each purchase order is tagged with a budget code, so the report shows what was spent from each budget line.
  • A code that is no longer used is switched off, not deleted, so old orders keep their code.

A small team: one person, two desks

The administrator gives one account two roles. The person sees both desks' work lists, and every action is still recorded under their own name.

PairWhat the person gets
Nurse and lab benchStarts at triage and also has the lab work list. The system no longer mistakes this person for a doctor.
Doctor and cashierThe doctor dashboard inside the full console, a "My cases" link, and the cashier screens.
Doctor and receptionCan register a patient, for example in an emergency at night, and then see them.
No cashier at allWith free care on, patients never wait at the cashier, so that desk is not needed for patient payments.

With second-person validation on, a lab result still needs a different person to confirm it, so a lab bench shared with nursing needs a second lab-trained person on the shift, or validation switched off.

Questions we need you to answer

1
Who is eligible?

Serving members, retired members, spouses, children (up to what age?), civilian staff, others? Is any of them only partly covered?

2
Will the hospital also see paying patients?

For example civilians from the area. If yes, free care should apply only to members and the others pay as usual.

3
What happens when a card is expired or cannot be checked?

Today the patient is treated and the reason is written. Should there be a limit, for example treat once and then require the card?

4
Is there a member list we can import?

A file from police HR with ID, name, rank, unit and card date would let reception verify members instead of typing them.

5
Which budget codes do you use?

Please share the Ministry of Finance or IFMIS chart of accounts codes the hospital spends from, so we load them for you.

6
Which price list values the care?

The hospital's own price list, or a government tariff? Medicines are valued at the latest purchase price unless you prefer another rule.

7
What must the monthly report look like?

Who receives it (Federal Police finance, the Ministry), who signs it, and is there an official form we should match?

8
How many staff work each shift?

With the numbers per desk, we can suggest which roles to pair for day, night and weekend shifts.

03 · Training and support

How a small Lihiq team trains and supports the whole hospital

The Lihiq team travels to the hospital, so time on site is planned in blocks. Hospital staff become the first line of support, and the Lihiq team stays reachable remotely between visits. The plan works with two Lihiq people: one trainer who also walks the floor, and one engineer who works remotely.

The plan, step by step

1
Before we travel

Lihiq loads accounts, the price list and catalogs remotely. The hospital names its super users and an IT focal person. Staff practise on the online demo with this guide and the walkthrough video.

2
Trip one: training, about two weeks on site

Super users are trained first and in depth. Then staff are trained in groups of desks that work on the same patient, two groups a day, with the super users helping.

3
Go-live: the team stays through the first outpatient week

One Lihiq person is on the floor during the day. A super user sits at each desk. The engineer is on call remotely in the evening and at night.

4
Later phases: wards and emergency, then theatre, store and finance

Super users train their own colleagues for each phase. Lihiq supports remotely, or with a short visit if a phase needs it.

5
After the first month

Remote support every day, and a planned visit each month for the first three months, then as agreed.

Training groups

One session covers several desks, so a single trainer reaches the whole hospital. Each group needs two or three sessions.

GroupDesks
FrontReception, cashier, triage
ClinicalDoctors, nurses, wards, emergency, theatre
DiagnosticsLaboratory, imaging
Medicines and storePharmacy, store, procurement and budget codes
ManagementAdministrator, finance, HR, reports and the free-care report

Who answers a question

1
This guide, desk cheat sheets and the demo

Most "how do I" questions are answered here, at any hour.

2
The super user on the shift

One or two per desk per shift, trained by Lihiq, on the floor with their colleagues.

3
The hospital IT focal person

Accounts and passwords, computers, printers, the network and the server.

4
The Lihiq team

Remotely by phone or a shared chat group, with secure access to the hospital server. Every issue goes into one shared log with how urgent it is, so nothing is lost.

What Lihiq does to cover more with fewer people

  • Every Lihiq team member is trained on every desk, so whoever is on site can help any desk.
  • Training is done in groups of desks, not desk by desk.
  • The hospital goes live in phases, so the team is never needed everywhere at once.
  • Fixes are made remotely, so the person on site stays with the staff.

What we need from the hospital

  • Names of super users: one or two per desk for each shift.
  • An IT focal person who is on site every day.
  • A training room with computers, and staff released from duty for their sessions.
  • Secure remote access to the hospital server for the Lihiq engineer.
  • The shift schedule, so night and weekend staff are trained too.

05 · How we test now

The test flow: reception, triage, doctor, laboratory, pharmacy

For this round of testing the cashier step is switched off, so the patient moves straight from one desk to the next. The charges are still recorded and wait for the cashier, who is tested in a later round.

1ReceptionRegisters the patient, picks the room and writes the complaint. The patient goes straight to triage.
2TriageTakes the vitals and a category. An abnormal reading is saved and flagged.
3DoctorExamines, diagnoses, and orders lab tests and medicine.
4LaboratoryCollects the specimen and posts the result without waiting for a receipt.
5PharmacyDispenses once the doctor has closed the case.

Step by step

  1. Reception: register a new patient with a name that starts with TEST, choose the room, write the complaint and press Register.
  2. Triage: open the patient from the triage queue, enter the vitals and a category, and save.
  3. Doctor: open the patient from your queue, write the examination, add a diagnosis and order a lab test.
  4. Laboratory: collect the specimen and post the result.
  5. Doctor: read the result, prescribe, and close the case.
  6. Pharmacy: open the prescription and dispense.

What you should see: each desk sees the patient only when it is their turn, no desk asks for a receipt, and the pharmacy records what was handed over. Stock counts are tested in a later round.

Before you start (administrator)

  • Facility workflow, Card fee: choose No card fee and save.
  • Facility workflow, Laboratory, imaging and medicine: choose Served first, billed later and save.
  • Leave triage on Required.
  • Use one account per desk: reception, nurse, doctor, laboratory and pharmacy.
Good to know: a patient already in today's queue is listed under Patients, Today, not under All patients. To see them under All patients too, open More filters and untick "Hide patients already in today's queue". Please send any problem to the test group with a screenshot and the name of the desk.
Switching back later: on the same Facility workflow page, choose Charged for the card fee and Paid before the service for orders. Each change applies to new check-ins and new orders from then on, and is recorded in the audit trail.

06 · Inpatient flow

A ward stay from start to end: decide, place, care, send and bring back, discharge

A stay is one record from admission to discharge. The patient keeps the same bed while away for an operation or a review, and every move is written in the stay's history with who did it and when.

1Doctor decidesFrom the visit, or from Emergency: the ward, the attending doctor and the reason. The decision waits for a bed.
2Ward placesThe ward nurse opens Wards, sees "Waiting for a bed" and taps a free bed. The bed shows as occupied.
3Care on the wardVitals, medication chart, fluid balance and nursing notes. Bed days go on the bill each day.
4Send to, bring backTheatre, Emergency, an outpatient room, Imaging, the Laboratory or another place. The bed is kept.
5DischargeHome, referred, left against advice or died, with notes. The summary prints and the bed is free.

Who does what

  1. Doctor: on the patient's visit, choose Admit, then the ward, the attending doctor and why. From Emergency, the disposition Admit does the same.
  2. Ward nurse: on Wards, under "Waiting for a bed", press Place and tap a free bed. The decided ward comes first; another ward is allowed when it is full.
  3. Nurse: open the bed's Stay. Chart vitals, give the medicines on the medication chart, chart intake and output, and write nursing notes.
  4. Ward nurse: to move the patient to another bed or ward, use "Move to another bed". The attending can change at the same time.
  5. Ward nurse: when the patient leaves the ward for a while, use Send to: choose the place and why. The bed shows Away with where and since when.
  6. Ward nurse: when the patient is back, press Back on the ward and note how they came back.
  7. Ward nurse: discharge with how the patient leaves and the notes. The discharge summary opens for printing.

Where a ward patient can go and come back

  • Theatre: for an operation. The theatre case is booked on the stay, so its charges join the stay's bill.
  • Emergency: when the patient deteriorates and needs the emergency team.
  • Outpatient room: for a specialist review. The patient joins that room's list for the doctor there, with no triage and no card fee.
  • Imaging and Laboratory: for a study or test that needs the patient there.
  • Another place: the nurse writes it, for example physiotherapy or dialysis.
  • The administrator chooses which of these places the wards offer, on Facility workflow, "Where a ward patient can go and come back".

Rules the system keeps

  • One open stay per patient. The stay is never copied when the patient moves.
  • While the patient is away, the bed stays theirs and cannot be given to someone else. A bed move waits until they are back.
  • A patient can be discharged from where they are, for example referred to another hospital straight from theatre.
  • If the administrator chooses, the stay's cash bill is settled before going home or being referred.
  • A discharge made by mistake can be undone within 48 hours, with a reason.

Before you start (administrator)

  • Facility workflow, Wards and admission: choose whether the ward may admit directly or only after a doctor decides.
  • Set up the wards and beds, and the outpatient rooms a ward can send patients to.
  • Tick the places in "Where a ward patient can go and come back".
  • Choose whether the bill is settled before discharge.
Good to know: on Wards, the Show filter has "Away from the ward" to list every patient who is out of their bed right now, with where they are.

Decisions we need from you

Send to works today as described above. These points can work in more than one way, so please tell us how your wards work. Each one becomes a setting the administrator can change, so different hospitals can choose differently.

1
Is the bed kept while the patient is away?

Today the bed is always kept, so the patient comes back to the same bed. Another option: free the bed while the patient is away, for example for a long stay in intensive care, and choose a bed again on return.

2
When a ward patient goes to Emergency, who looks after them there?

Today the ward only records that the patient is in Emergency. Another option: the patient also appears on the Emergency board, so the emergency team charts vitals and notes there until the patient comes back.

3
Imaging and the Laboratory: does the doctor's order send the patient?

Today the nurse chooses Send to by hand. Another option: when imaging or the laboratory calls an admitted patient for an order, the patient is marked away automatically, and back when the study or sample is done.

4
Theatre: is the patient marked away when the operation starts?

Today the nurse sends the patient to theatre by hand. Another option: starting the operation in Theatre marks the patient away, and the ward confirms the return after recovery.

5
An outpatient review: should the specialist's fee be charged?

Today the patient joins the room's list with no card fee, and anything the specialist orders follows the hospital's payment setting. Should a consultation fee for the review be added to the stay's bill?

6
How do doctors write the daily ward round?

Today the attending doctor works on the admission visit, opened from the stay with "Open visit". Do your doctors want a separate daily progress note and a list of their own ward patients on their dashboard?

7
Who may send a patient out and bring them back?

Today anyone who can manage the wards. Should this be limited, for example to the ward nurse in charge, or need the attending doctor's agreement?

07 · Abrihot

Abrihot: the Adult and Child units on Lihiq

Abrihot is a psychology center, so its day is not a hospital day. A client is screened or assessed, matched with a therapist, and then comes for sessions. The same Lihiq runs it: an administrator switches the facility from Hospital to Therapy center and everything below appears. A hospital sees no change.

1ContactThe client calls, walks in, is referred, or fills the online form. The front desk logs it, with the parent for a child.
2Screening or assessmentThe clinical director assigns it and books the time. Adult: a free 20-minute screening. Child: an assessment.
3Result and matchThe professional writes the result. The director matches a therapist and a program (Sessional, or RITP for children).
4SessionsConsent, then one session or a weekly series, paid before. Notes, therapy plan, ILP and progress evaluation.
5EndingTherapy report, assessment report, consultation, or referral, with a follow-up date.

Working on the test server now

  • Case board per unit that replaces the Adult and Child client trackers, with stage counts, search and an Excel download.
  • Booking of the screening or assessment and of every session, with a clash check on the therapist's time.
  • Fees go to the cashier, and with "paid before" on a session cannot be marked attended until it is paid.
  • Session notes, plans and reports are clinical: only the client's therapists and the clinical director read them. A plan is drafted, sent to the director and reviewed.
  • Senior and junior therapist on a child's case, and a change of therapist at any time with a reason.
  • Online registration form in English and Amharic, which replaces the Google Form. Each answer waits for the front desk to make it a case.
  • Referral letters are filed unopened by the front desk; only the director opens them.
  • Coded client names for managers and the quality lead, and a report with each unit's funnel, waiting time, no-show rate and each therapist's load.

Not built yet

  • The center's own screening and assessment result forms. Today the form is generic: concern, summary, risk, recommendation.
  • Consent signed on a tablet. Today the front desk records who signed.
  • Sending the form link by SMS or Telegram from Lihiq. The administrator copies the link.
  • Package or monthly billing (for example a monthly RITP fee). Today each session is its own charge.
  • Staff screens in Amharic. The online form already has Amharic.
  • Video sessions.
For the administrator: Facility workflow, Kind of facility, choose Therapy center. Then set each unit's first step, session length and fee, whether a session is paid before, and whether consent comes first. The same page opens the online form and gives its link. In Access, create people as Clinical director, Therapist or Therapy center manager.

Questions for Abrihot

We read the SOPs, the therapy cycle and the welcome flyer and answered what they already tell us. For each point below, please correct us if we are wrong. Where we have no answer, we need it from Abrihot.

1
Your forms and trackers

We need from Abrihot: a blank copy of the Google Form and of the Adult unit, Child unit, Therapist communication and Child sessional trackers, so we match every field and column. The SOPs name these trackers but do not list their columns.

2
The result forms

We need from Abrihot: the screening result form, the assessment result form and the referral form. The SOP says they are sent by e-mail as a file that can be copied and pasted. Until we have them, Lihiq uses a generic result: concern, summary, risk, recommendation.

3
Prices and payment

From your documents: the adult screening is free (flyer), and the client pays in advance, with the front desk checking before each session (SOP). Lihiq works this way now.

We need from Abrihot: the session fee for each unit, whether the Child assessment is free like the adult screening, and whether RITP is paid by session, by package or monthly.

4
Change of therapist

From your documents: a client can change therapist at any time and can contact the client case manager for help (flyer). Lihiq lets the clinical director make the change, with a reason, and keeps the history.

We need from Abrihot: whether the case manager may also approve it.

5
Online sessions

From your documents: nothing mentions video. We assume sessions are in person, so Lihiq has no video link.

We need from Abrihot: confirmation.

6
Consent

From your documents: the client signs a consent form before choosing a time and paying (SOP). Lihiq records who signed and will not book a session before that.

We need from Abrihot: whether it is signed on paper or on a tablet. We suggest paper first, recorded by the front desk.

7
Reports

From your documents: the front desk reports to the operations manager, the clinical directors to the general manager, and the service quality lead to the general manager and the executive (SOP). Lihiq's report counts each unit's contacts, waiting time, matches, no-shows and therapist load, for any date range, with Excel.

We need from Abrihot: one report they use today, so we match its layout.

8
Who sees names

From your documents: the flyer promises that client information is coded and protected. We assume the operations manager, the service quality lead and the general manager see a client code, not the name. Front desk, the director and the client's own therapists see names.

We need from Abrihot: confirmation.

9
The online form

From your documents: the Google Form sent through the client's preferred app is the first step today (flyer). We suggest using both in the first weeks, then moving to the Lihiq link when the front desk is comfortable.

We need from Abrihot: agreement.

09 · Administrator

What your administrator controls

These change how the hospital works, without a programmer. Each change is recorded in the audit trail.

Workflow switches

  • Card fee on or off, and whether it is charged before or after triage.
  • Triage required or skipped; queue order (Red first, by category, or by arrival).
  • Pay before a service, or bill later.
  • What CBHI, fee-waiver and exempt patients are spared: the card fee only, or all services.
  • Which registration fields are required; the Pediatrics age limit.
  • Admission by a doctor's decision first, or directly at the ward desk.
  • Settle the bill before discharge; cashier shifts required; the cashier's discount limit.
  • Second-person validation of lab results; theatre safety checklist required.
  • SMS appointment reminders, and how many hours ahead.
  • Free care on or off, the sponsor's name, and the list of member types.

Accounts and catalogs

  • Roles and permissions per desk, and one named account per person. One account can hold two roles when staff cover two desks.
  • An account locks after 5 wrong passwords; the administrator can unlock it.
  • When the administrator sets a password, the person must choose their own at the next sign-in.
  • Passwords need 10 or more characters with letters and a number.
  • Prices and services, lab tests and their normal ranges, drugs, rooms, wards and stores.
  • The hospital's own list of drug interaction rules, kept with the head pharmacist.
  • Catalogs can be imported in bulk from a file.

10 · Safety and records

How records and data are protected

Records

  • Every change is in the audit trail, with who did it and when.
  • Signed records are never overwritten. A correction is added beside the original, with the reason.
  • A closed case can get an addendum, or be reopened by a doctor with a reason.
  • A critical lab result is texted to the ordering doctor once an SMS provider is set up.
  • Patients see only their own record; patient accounts can never open staff screens.
  • On the patient's phone, the sign-in is kept in the phone's secure storage and is replaced each time the app uses it. If an old copy is ever used, that sign-in is closed and the patient signs in again.

Server and backup

  • Runs on your own server and network. Daily work needs no internet.
  • An encrypted backup every night, opened straight away to prove it can be read, kept for 30 days.
  • A monthly restore check compares the backup with the live system.
  • A health check reports when the system stops answering, the disk is 85% full, or no backup was made in 26 hours.
  • Demo accounts are switched off at installation, and the system refuses to start with a weak secret key.
  • On the test server: secure web address (HTTPS) only, the database is not reachable from the internet, server sign-in by key only, and repeated failed sign-ins are blocked automatically.

12 · Next steps

What was added, what comes next, and the road to go-live

The first eight items were built and tested on 16 October 2026, the next two on 18 and 22 October, and the police hospital items on 26 October, each with its own automated tests. The test server went online on 3 November. The rest are planned and depend on what the pilot needs.

Road to go-live

1
Hospital server in place

About 8 cores, 32 GB memory, two mirrored SSDs of at least 500 GB, a UPS and a backup disk. A starting point, not a quotation.

2
Installation and your data

We install the system and import your price list, test and drug catalogs and staff list. You name an administrator, and every staff member gets their own account.

3
Training

Super users are trained first, then staff in groups of desks, with short desk guides. See Training and support.

4
Acceptance test

Your staff run the sixteen scenarios on this page. We run a load test and a security review on your server.

5
Pilot go-live

Outpatient plus one ward first, then the rest of the hospital.

Optional: an SMS provider account turns on appointment reminders and critical-result texts.
