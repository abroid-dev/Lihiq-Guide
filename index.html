04 · Administrator

What your administrator controls

These change how the hospital works, without a programmer. Each change is recorded in the audit trail.

Workflow switches

  • Card fee on or off, and whether it is charged before or after triage.
  • Triage required or skipped; queue order (Red first, by category, or by arrival).
  • Pay before a service, or bill later.
  • What CBHI, fee-waiver and exempt patients are spared: the card fee only, or all services.
  • Which registration fields are required; the Pediatrics age limit.
  • Admission by a doctor's decision first, or directly at the ward desk.
  • Settle the bill before discharge; cashier shifts required; the cashier's discount limit.
  • Second-person validation of lab results; theatre safety checklist required.
  • SMS appointment reminders, and how many hours ahead.

Accounts and catalogs

  • Roles and permissions per desk, and one named account per person.
  • An account locks after 5 wrong passwords; the administrator can unlock it.
  • When the administrator sets a password, the person must choose their own at the next sign-in.
  • Passwords need 10 or more characters with letters and a number.
  • Prices and services, lab tests and their normal ranges, drugs, rooms, wards and stores.
  • Catalogs can be imported in bulk from a file.

05 · Safety and records

How records and data are protected

Records

  • Every change is in the audit trail, with who did it and when.
  • Signed records are never overwritten. A correction is added beside the original, with the reason.
  • A closed case can get an addendum, or be reopened by a doctor with a reason.
  • A critical lab result is texted to the ordering doctor once an SMS provider is set up.
  • Patients see only their own record; patient accounts can never open staff screens.

Server and backup

  • Runs on your own server and network. Daily work needs no internet.
  • An encrypted backup every night, opened straight away to prove it can be read, kept for 30 days.
  • A monthly restore check compares the backup with the live system.
  • A health check reports when the system stops answering, the disk is 85% full, or no backup was made in 26 hours.
  • Demo accounts are switched off at installation, and the system refuses to start with a weak secret key.

07 · Next steps

What was added, what comes next, and the road to go-live

The first eight items were built and tested on 16 October 2026, each with its own automated tests. The rest are planned and depend on what the pilot needs.

Road to go-live

1
Hospital server in place

About 8 cores, 32 GB memory, two mirrored SSDs of at least 500 GB, a UPS and a backup disk. A starting point, not a quotation.

2
Installation and your data

We install the system and import your price list, test and drug catalogs and staff list. You name an administrator, and every staff member gets their own account.

3
Training

Desk leads are trained desk by desk, with short desk guides.

4
Acceptance test

Your staff run the fourteen scenarios on this page. We run a load test and a security review on your server.

5
Pilot go-live

Outpatient plus one ward first, then the rest of the hospital.

Optional: an SMS provider account turns on appointment reminders and critical-result texts.
